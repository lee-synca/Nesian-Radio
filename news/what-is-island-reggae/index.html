What Is Island Reggae?

Island reggae is the warm, soulful, harmony-rich style of reggae that defines the modern Pacific sound. It takes the laid-back rhythm and heartbeat bass of Jamaican reggae and blends it with island melodies, close vocal harmonies and Pacific storytelling — the result is music that feels like sunshine, family and home all at once.

Where does island reggae come from?

Reggae travelled from Jamaica across the Pacific through the 1970s and 80s, and island communities made it their own. In Samoa, Tonga, Fiji, Hawaii, Aotearoa (New Zealand) and beyond, local artists took reggae's relaxed groove and layered it with the vocal harmonies and lyrical themes that run deep in island culture — love, faith, family and the islands themselves.

What makes it different?

If you've heard island reggae, you'll recognise a few signatures:

  • Rich vocal harmonies — often sung by groups and families, a hallmark of Pacific music
  • A softer, sun-soaked groove — mellow guitars, gentle keys and a rolling bassline
  • Heartfelt, positive lyrics — songs about love, home, culture and community
  • Island language and identity — pride in where the music comes from

Why island reggae matters

For Pacific Islanders — especially those living far from the islands — island reggae is more than a genre. It's a connection to home, a soundtrack to family gatherings, church, long drives and celebrations. It carries culture across generations and oceans.
